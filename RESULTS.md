# Results

Regenerated by `scripts/report.py` from the JSON each run writes; none of it is typed in by hand. Sections are split by what they are: the false-alarm rates come from real cells, the lead times come from a fault injected into a simulation.

## The data: 76 real cells from two laboratories

- **Severson et al. 2019 (TRI/Stanford/MIT fast-charging dataset), batch1** -- A123 APR18650M1A, LFP/graphite 18650, 1.1 Ah nominal, 3.3 V, 30 C forced convection. I [A], V [V], T [degC] (thermocouple on the can), 5 s sampling. 46 cells.
- **NASA Ames PCoE Battery Data Set (Saha & Goebel)** -- 18650 Li-ion, LCO/graphite, 2 Ah nominal, 4 / 24 / 43 C, still air, varies by cell. I [A], V [V], T [degC] (thermocouple), ~19 s sampling. 30 cells.

Constant-current windows are cut out of every kept cycle. They differ in the one way that turns out to decide most of the result: every cell in the Severson dataset is discharged identically at 4C, while the Severson charge policies and the NASA discharges vary from cell to cell in both current and chamber temperature.

| window | protocol | windows | cells | length | \|I\| range | distinct current profiles | median peak rise over ambient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Severson 4C discharge (identical duty cycle for every cell) | 4C constant current to 2.0 V, identical for every cell | 1285 | 46 | 780 s @ 5 s | 2.5-4.0 A | 2 | 4.7 K |
| Severson fast charge (each cell's own 3.6C-8C policy) | first 200 s of the cell's fast-charge policy, constant current at 3.6C-8C (some policies step down to a second rate inside the window) | 1221 | 46 | 200 s @ 5 s | 3.0-8.0 A | 28 | 3.7 K |
| NASA PCoE discharge (1-4 A, chamber at 4 / 24 / 43 C) | constant-current discharge, 1-4 A depending on the cell | 1211 | 30 | 900 s @ 20 s | 1.0-4.0 A | 11 | 6.6 K |

## The twin, and whether it tracks a real cell

An SPMe with a lumped thermal submodel, six identified scalars, fitted separately for each window set on the fit cells and scored on cells it never saw. PyBAMM's `Prada2013` LFP/graphite chemistry is used for the Severson cells and `Ramadass2004` LCO for the NASA cells. The reduced-order twin this repo started with -- two parameters, `C dT/dt = R I^2 - hA (T - T_amb)`, fitted on the same windows by the same trajectory criterion -- is scored alongside it.

### Energy balance, checked rather than assumed

All the heat a cell releases has to come out of the gap between its open-circuit and terminal voltage. PyBAMM's default heat terms close only about half of that at these rates, which is why heat of mixing is switched on and the contact resistance's own dissipation is added by hand.

## Lead time under a simulated internal short

> **Simulated.** No public cycling dataset carries a labelled internal-short onset, so the fault is injected into the physics twin and every number in this section is a simulation result. What the detector sees is a **real measured temperature trace from a real held-out cell** plus the increment the short causes, so the alarm has to clear that cell's real residual noise floor. The threshold is the one calibrated on real healthy cells above and is never re-tuned here.

### Severson 4C discharge (identical duty cycle for every cell)

120 real held-out windows from 24 cells, cell-registered threshold at alpha=0.05 (residual 1.59 K, no-model 3.57 K).

## What it costs to run

Single core, no parallelism. The real-time factor is what decides whether the twin can run online next to the cell rather than in a nightly batch.

| window | simulated span | PyBAMM twin | faster than real time | reduced-order twin | faster than real time | 1000-cell fleet, per cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Severson 4C discharge | 780 s | 0.55 s | 1426x | 0.43 ms | 1,810,691x | 547 core-s |
| Severson fast charge | 200 s | 0.38 s | 526x | 0.11 ms | 1,752,190x | 380 core-s |
| NASA PCoE discharge | 900 s | 0.80 s | 1128x | 0.13 ms | 6,975,453x | 798 core-s |
